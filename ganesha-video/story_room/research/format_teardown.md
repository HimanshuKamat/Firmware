## 1. Cross-cutting evidence

- **Pace.** Labs define "fast" differently: scene change every 4 s vs 15 s (Cooper 2009); every 11 s (SpongeBob) vs 34 s (Caillou) (Lillard & Peterson 2011) [S32]. Nine minutes of fast cartoon lowered 4-year-olds' executive-function scores versus drawing [S32]. Anderson 1977 found pace alone did not matter once content was controlled; Rose 2022 found an effect at age 3 but not 4 [S32]. 73% of 426 toddler-watched YouTube videos were fast-paced [S32]. No study yet tests online-video pacing [S32]. Strong: pace and attention link in short lab tests. Weak: long-term harm. Myth: that one cut rate is "safe".
- **Interaction.** Participation cues helped learning only for children already familiar with the show (n=187, Dora, Piotrowski 2014) [S33]. Blue's Clues repeated one episode five days running so children could join in [S34].
- **Platform.** Made-for-kids turns off cards, end screens, comments, notifications, personalised ads and Save to playlist [S35]. The 15 Jul 2025 "inauthentic content" rule bars "mass-produced, generic, repetitive" content, including templated AI without original perspective [S36]. On 1 Apr 2026, 200+ groups asked YouTube to ban AI content from YouTube Kids; YouTube said it limits AI there to "a small set of high-quality channels" [S37].

## 3. Our film against these formats (measured values from the project files)

**Strong.** One premise, one rule, one turn, one payoff, no B-story: Bluey-like [S8, I]. The refrain appears four times (chant x3, then the song), the familiarity participation needs [S33]. Average net shot 8.2 s (6-10 s, 31 shots) cannot be compared with lab "scene change" intervals [S32], since our locations change far less often than shots [I]; Rose found no pace effect at age 4 [S32]. Cut rate is probably not the weakness, though motion level was never measured. Speech fills 44% of runtime. The title uses Ms Rachel's keyword-and-pipe pattern [S41].

**Deviates.**
1. Six seconds of logo before content; story proper at 0:27. Song-first formats open on content [I].
2. Song at 4:02 (86% through), four lines, 13 s of lyrics on screen. Ms Rachel interleaves songs throughout [S5].
3. One movement prompt (flap, 1:42). Pinkfong, Cosmic Kids and Ms Rachel centre movement. The story's own "three circles" is not used as a body prompt.
4. Seven participation cues, one every ~40 s, with ~2 s pauses and no designed response beat.
5. Lesson card at about 3:57, against Brumm's "no learning this or that" [S7]. Mild.
6. Uniform rhythm: 32 identical 0.5 s crossfades; 25 s of stills at the emotional payoff.
7. The 20 s end screen (7% of runtime) cannot be clickable on made-for-kids [S35]. This confirms the metadata's uncertain note.
8. "Mooshak" collides with Bal Ganesh [S28]. YouTube search not checked [U].

**Five format changes for the next film**
1. Cold open: logo 3 s or less, first participation cue by 0:15.
2. Tease the refrain melody at about 1:00 and 2:30, then close with 3 repeats (25-35 s), each with one gesture (a hug for "hold your family near").
3. Make each question a 4 s pause beat with a looking-at-you cue; add two movement prompts from story actions (turn three times, fly).
4. Vary shots, floor 3 s, average 6-8 s: reaction and action cuts for the race, long calm holds elsewhere; write beats to clip length (report C6).
5. Say the lesson once and show it; 8-10 s end card with a next-story tease; link a playlist in the description.

**Five title and thumbnail patterns** (respectful, gentle; thumbnails [I])
1. "[Story] | A [Deity or Festival] Story for Kids". One calm face, at most 3 words.
2. "Who Will Win the Golden Mango? | Mooshak's Story Time". The question image, not the spoiler.
3. "Count the Modaks with Mooshak | Counting Story for Toddlers". Big numerals, Mooshak pointing.
4. "Mooshak's Story Time #2: [Story]". Fixed frame and mascot corner for series recognition.
5. "[Festival] Story for Kids | [Story]" released two weeks early [I]. Warm palette, no shocked faces, no deity in distress.

## How the Story Room should use this

1. Open on content within 3 s of the logo; ask for the first participation by 0:15.
2. One premise, one rule, one turn, no B-story, 3-5 minutes. Bluey is the structure model; Super Simple Songs and Ms Rachel are the pacing models.
3. Give every refrain one gesture and repeat it at least 3 times, teased earlier, so children know it before the finale (Piotrowski 2014).
4. Write each question as a 4 s pause beat, and build movement from the story's own verbs (circle, fly, hug).
5. Shots 3-10 s, average 6-8 s, calm scenes long; no study gives a safe number [I]. Avoid CoComelon-style cuts, hour compilations and autoplay blocks.
6. Show lessons through action, state them once, keep the end card to 8-10 s, and assume no clickable end screen on made-for-kids.
7. Protect the channel from the "AI slop" label: varied stories, human-written scripts and review, a named cultural reviewer for every deity story, no templated sameness, and a modest cadence (1-2 films a month [I]).
8. Before locking a name or title, check "Mooshak" against Bal Ganesh and YouTube search; reject clickbait faces and deities in distress.
