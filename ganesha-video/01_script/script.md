# Ganesha's Big Race – script (draft 1, for Checkpoint 1)

**Title card:** Ganesha's Big Race – A Ganesha Story for Kids
**Lesson, said clearly near the end:** "Our family is our whole world."
**Runtime:** 4:40 (hard cap 5:00) · **Words:** 310 (narration, character lines, chant and song) · **Shots:** 33
**Format:** 16:9, 1920×1080, 24 fps · **Narration speed used for timing:** 115 words per minute, short sentences of 4–8 words

---

## 1. Story options

| # | Story | One line |
|---|-------|----------|
| 1 | **Ganesha and Kartikeya's race around the world** *(recommended)* | Kartikeya zooms off on his peacock; Ganesha calmly walks around his parents, because they are his whole world, and wins. Lesson: our family is our whole world. |
| 2 | Ganesha and the Moon | Ganesha tumbles after too many modaks and the Moon laughs. Lesson: be kind when someone falls. In some tellings Ganesha curses the Moon, which is too harsh for this age, so it would need a soft, new ending. |
| 3 | Ganesha and Kubera's feast | Proud Kubera throws a grand feast and Ganesha eats it all, still hungry, until a humble offering made with love fills him up. Lesson: love matters more than riches. Many characters and food shots, so it is the busiest to animate. |

**Why #1:** one simple idea, big clear emotions, easy to count and repeat ("round and round"), a clean one-line lesson, and it uses your cast exactly (Ganesha, Mooshak, Kartikeya on his peacock, Shiva, Parvati).

## 3. Repetition and interaction map

- **Chant ★ "Ganpati Bappa… Morya!"** comes back 4 times: shot 04 (taught: "Say it with me!"), shot 19 (Ganesha sets off), shot 26 (the win), shot 32 (the goodbye song).
- **Mooshak 🐭 "Squeak-squeak!"** comes back 4 times: shots 02, 09, 16 and 33.
- **Three question moments, each with a 2-second gap:** shot 09 count the modaks (1-2-3), shot 14 flap your arms like a peacock, shots 20–21 count Ganesha's circles (1-2-3).
- **Lesson** (shot 30, with the lesson card): "Our family is our whole world." It returns in the song: "They are your whole world, dear!"

- Under the 5:00 cap by **20 s**. I kept that margin on purpose, so a slower narrator or a longer shot can't push the video over.
- The brief's timings (hook to 0:20, lesson to 4:45) would add up to about 5:10 once the 6 s intro and 20 s end screen are included. So the hook runs 0:06–0:27, the title card sits on top of the first hook shot, and the song's last chorus plays under the end screen.
- Every shot with words was checked: speaking time at 115 wpm, plus a breath between lines, plus each 2 s gap, plus 1 s of fade room, fits inside the shot. The tightest shots have about 0.3 s to spare.

**Word count: 310, below your 450–550 guide. This is deliberate.** 450 words at 115 wpm is 3:55 of non-stop speech. Add the 2 s gaps, a breath between sentences and the 0.5 s fade room on each shot, and it needs about 296 s of the 274 s available (300 s minus the intro and end screen). It cannot fit under 5:00, and toddlers would get no quiet moments. If you want denser narration, tell me and I'll add shots, but I would not recommend it.

## 6. Story details I want you to confirm

I told the story in the most widely told form I know. Please check these against your family's tradition:

1. **The prize is a golden mango.** In the versions I know best, a divine fruit (often a mango) is the prize. Some tellings use a different fruit or a modak. If your tradition differs, tell me and I'll swap it. It only touches shots 10–12 and 25–28.
2. **"Brother", not "big brother" or "little brother".** Tellings disagree on who is older, so I avoided it.
3. **The kind ending is my addition.** Ganesha sharing the mango with Kartikeya and the two hugging is not in the traditional text I know. In many tellings Kartikeya is upset and leaves, which is too sad for ages 4–5. Tell me if you would rather end it differently.
4. **"Three times around the world"** and Ganesha explaining that his parents are his world are the classic core of the story. I kept both.
5. **Narada** (the sage who brings the fruit in some tellings) is left out, to keep the cast small. The mango simply appears in Baba's hands.
6. **Amma and Baba** are used as the children's names for Parvati and Shiva. For Marathi, "Aai" and "Baba" would replace "Amma".
