1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
====================================================================================================

What was asked: act as producer, writer and editor of a 4 to 5 minute (never over 5:00) animated
children's video for toddlers (4 to 5 years old), 16:9, 1920x1080, with the lesson "Our family is our
whole world." Also a 60 s vertical (9:16) Shorts teaser, captions, thumbnails and a YouTube package.

What was made: "Ganesha's Big Race", a gentle story in which Shiva offers a golden mango to whichever
son circles the world three times first. Kartikeya flies off on his peacock; little Ganesha instead
walks three times around his parents, "because they are my whole world", and wins. A small mouse,
Mooshak, is the child-facing guide and comic relief. The video has counting games (modaks, circles),
a "flap your arms" prompt, the chant "Ganpati Bappa... Morya!", an original sung goodbye song and a
20 s end screen.

Result [M]: 280.0 s (4:40), 1920x1080, 24 fps, H.264 High, AAC 320 kbps 48 kHz, loudness -14.0 LUFS
integrated, true peak -1.5 dBTP, loudness range 8.2 LU, no black frames and no frozen frames found by
ffmpeg scans. Plus a 56 s Shorts teaser, 63-cue SRT captions, three 1280x720 thumbnails and a YouTube
title / description / tags / chapters package. Delivered as a 91 MB one-file MP4, a 408 MiB master
(split in five parts for GitHub) and a 73 MB extras zip.

Cost [E]: Krea about 66,000 credit units (about USD 115 at the Max plan's USD 0.00175 per unit),
ElevenLabs about 12,250 credits (about USD 2.45), Canva 0, HyperFrames 0 (run locally). Over 97 percent
of the money went to AI video clips on Krea. Wall-clock time: about 10 hours of elapsed time, including
waits for the owner and a container restart.

The five findings a reviewer should read first
----------------------------------------------
 1. MODEL CHOICE COST ABOUT USD 55. Seedance 2.5 clips cost about 3.2x more than Veo 3.1 clips at 1080p
    (3,791 vs 1,185 units; quoted by Krea). Twelve clips used Seedance, about 31,000 units more than
    the same twelve on Veo. The plan assumed the opposite and nobody had a price until the balance ran
    out mid-batch. The cheapest fix is a free price quote BEFORE planning (Section 15A).
 2. ABOUT 9 PERCENT OF THE RUNTIME IS NOT ANIMATED. Shots 23, 27 and 28 (the "how did you win",
    mango-sharing and hug beats) are slow zooms on still pictures because Veo's content filter refused
    to animate them. They were originally planned for Seedance and were moved to Veo to save credits.
    Re-doing them on Seedance would cost about 11,400 units (about USD 20) at 1080p (Section 15B).
 3. NOTHING WAS HEARD BY A HUMAN. Narration, music, the sung song and 41 sound effects were placed by
    calculation and measured, never auditioned. Pronunciation of "Ganpati Bappa Morya", "Kartikeya",
    "modak" has not been checked by an Indian-English listener (Section 15C).
 4. 16 OF 17 VEO CLIPS WERE SLOWED DOWN (by 6 to 20 percent) to fill the narration, and two end with
    held frames. The slow-down duplicates frames (no interpolation), so motion can feel floaty or
    slightly juddery. Designing each shot to 7.5 s of net time, or interpolating, avoids it (15B).
 5. THE ONE-FILE DOWNLOAD IS A CONVENIENCE COPY (2.6 Mbps). For upload to YouTube the 12 Mbps master
    should be used. The master exists only as five 90 MiB parts in the repository (Section 15D).

Overall verdict (my own, to be challenged): the project meets every hard requirement in the brief and is
a complete, uploadable package. It is "good and consistent", not yet "premium": characters are
on-model and the look is unified, but there is no lip-sync, three beats are static, the audio has not
been heard, and the cut is uniformly slow with 32 identical 0.5 s crossfades.

   said they would be unavailable and asked the assistant to finish autonomously.
 - Tool stack named in the brief: Krea (images, video), ElevenLabs (voice, music, effects), Vivideo
   (video prompt builder), HyperFrames (graphics), Canva (thumbnails).
 - Respect: Hindu sacred figures (Ganesha, Shiva, Parvati, Kartikeya) treated reverently; comedy only
   from the mouse.

5. PHASE-BY-PHASE ACCOUNT
====================================================================================================
Dates are UTC. Project start 2026-10-05 20:58; final encode finished 2026-10-06 07:08.

PHASE 0  Setup check
   Confirmed that Vivideo has the style animation_3d_character, that Krea has Seedance 2.5, Veo 3.1,
   Kling 3.0 and Nano Banana Pro, and that ElevenLabs has Indian-English voices and eleven_v3. Network
   to the Krea and ElevenLabs file hosts was blocked at first and had to be opened by the owner.

PHASE 1  Script, shot list, bible  (CHECKPOINT 1, approved)
   - 01_script/script.md: 63 lines (L01 to L63), 310 words, 33 shots, 4:40.
   - 01_script/shotlist.md: per shot action, camera, emotion, tool, notes.
   - 01_script/bible.md: character, location and iconography text blocks (used word for word in every
     prompt so characters stay on-model).
   - Choices the owner approved: Ganesha has two arms; Kartikeya has one face; Shiva has a tiny friendly
     cobra and a closed third eye; the prize is a golden mango; Ganesha shares it with his brother.

PHASE 2  Prompts  (no credits)
   - 02_prompts/prompts.json: for each shot, a Vivideo-built motion prompt plus the bible blocks,
     location, props and negatives (recipe in Appendix A). Defaults: 16:9, 1920x1080, 24 fps, audio off,
     2 versions per shot, 2 retries per prompt (the 2-versions rule was later dropped to save credits).

PHASE 3a  References  (CHECKPOINT 2a)
   - 36 Nano Banana Pro images (4 options each for 6 character sheets and 3 locations), plus one Parvati
     re-render (accidentally submitted at 2K instead of 1K). 9 references approved (03_refs/PICKS.md).

PHASE 3b  Keyframes  (CHECKPOINT 2)
   - One 1K image per shot (32) using the references. Drift was found in several shots (Ganesha's crown
     and skin tone, a thatched hut that does not exist in the world, Shiva in leopard print, one shot far
     too dark, an extra child). Fixed with extra preamble clauses and up to two redo rounds (shots 18,
     21, 23, 29). Shot 21 kept with a residual hut mismatch (the owner accepted it). 76 Krea images in
     total, 41 of which are in the final reference/keyframe set.

PHASE 3c  Clips  (CHECKPOINT 3)
   - Three price tests (Seedance 480p draft, Seedance 1080p, Veo 1080p; two were reused in the cut).
   - The owner upgraded Pro to Max, asked for no more test runs. 14 clips were submitted as one batch
     (11 Seedance, 3 Veo). Balance then hit about 495 units and the next requests were refused with
     INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE. The refused requests returned the real prices (Section 4); work stopped.
   - After a top-up, the remaining 16 shots were submitted as Veo 3.1 1080p 8 s, start image only
     (about 3x cheaper). Veo's content filter rejected three shots (23, 27, 28: all show Kartikeya with
     Ganesha; not charged). Those three became ffmpeg push-ins on the approved stills (owner's "option 3").
   - Shot 31 came back with an unwanted extra girl at the frame edge; it was regenerated through
     ElevenLabs' Veo 3.1 Fast with a locked five-person cast and a static camera (clean first time).

PHASE 4  Voice, SFX, music  (CHECKPOINT 4)
   - Narrator audition: 3 voices read the same 38 words (Ria 108 wpm, Arjun 126, Sahana 134). Sahana was
     chosen. 59 lines were generated, one take each (49 narrator, 7 Mooshak, 3 Ganesha). The 4 sung lines
     come from the song. Total speech 124.4 s.
   - The placeholder 6 s intro was removed from the working timeline at the owner's request, so the whole
     working timeline ran 6 s earlier (shot 02 at 0:00) and +6 s was added back at final assembly.
   - Sound effects v1 (12 files) came out only 1 to 2 s long each, so the owner asked for better effects.
     Sound effects v2 (16 files incl. three 30 s ambience beds) replaced them.
   - Music: a 225 s story bed, a 20 s evening bed, a 38 s sung song. The song was cut (7.5 s = 10 beats
     removed between the two "Morya" lines) so it reads verse, Morya, Morya.

PHASE 5  Graphics  (autonomous)
   - 11 HyperFrames projects (Section 8). Nine author agents launched through a workflow tool hit the
     account's session limit and failed; the assistant wrote and reviewed the overlays by hand using
     contact sheets (07_graphics/preview.py).

PHASE 6  Final assembly (autonomous)
   - Picture timeline (build_base.py), numeral removal in shot 21 (fix_shot21.py), shot 31 swap, shot 33
     extension to 20 s, overlays composited (build_final.py), audio mix and two-pass loudness
     normalisation (build_audio_final.py), final encode, black / freeze / loudness checks.

PHASE 7  Extras and delivery
   - Shorts teaser (10 moments + end card, 9:16), captions, three thumbnails, Canva design, YouTube
     metadata, summary. GitHub rejects files over 100 MB, so: master split in 90 MiB parts, a 91 MB
     one-file copy and a 73 MB zip were added so the owner could download to a laptop.

7.3 Sound effects
   - v1: 12 clips, 200 credits, discarded because each was only 1 to 2 s long whatever length was asked.
   - v2: 16 clips (three 30 s ambience beds: morning, sky, evening; wing flaps, footsteps, mouse scamper,
     claps, cheer, giggle, mango shimmer, hop, fruit split, three squeak variants, a 6 s intro sting),
     513 credits actual. Built by 06_audio/build_sfx_v2.py: seamless crossfaded loops of the beds, wing

   - Narration (centred), SFX (centred), music beds (stereo), song (stereo, -6 dB), intro sting.
   - Music beds are ducked by the narration with sidechaincompress (threshold 0.03, ratio 3, attack
     20 ms, release 600 ms). The sung song and the SFX are not ducked.
   - alimiter at 0.89, then two-pass loudnorm with linear=true to -14 LUFS / -1 dBTP.
   - Result [M]: -14.0 LUFS integrated, -1.5 dBTP, LRA 8.2. Intermediate WAV 48 kHz 24-bit stereo.

15A. CREDIT AND COST OPTIMISATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C1  Default to Veo 3.1; use Seedance only where Veo cannot do the job.
    Veo 1080p 8 s = 1,185 units; Seedance 1080p 9 s = 3,791 (3.2x). The 12 Seedance clips cost about
    46,000 units; as 8 s Veo clips they would cost about 14,200. Saving about 31,000 units (about USD 55,
    nearly half the Krea bill). Trade-offs: Veo is capped at 8 s (so the slow-down issue, 15B item B2); Veo
    takes a start image only (in this project the start image was enough to keep characters on-model);
    Veo refused three shots. Effort: none, a planning rule. Confidence: high on the price, medium on the
    quality equivalence.

C2  Get the price before planning.
    Krea shows no price, but a refused request returns a free quote. Do a deliberately under-funded
    request for each model at the start of the project and write the prices into the plan. This alone
    would have avoided: the wrong early estimate (about 1,800 per clip), the mid-batch stop, three
    extra owner round-trips for top-ups and the Max upgrade timing. Effort: 5 minutes.

C3  Test Seedance at 720p.
    720p 9 s = 1,541 units vs 3,791 at 1080p: about 59 percent cheaper. At 110 s of Seedance footage
    that is about 27,500 units (about USD 48). The final video is 1080p, so the 720p clips would need
    upscaling; whether that is acceptable for flat-shaded 3D cartoon art is untested [U]. Run one shot
    both ways and compare before deciding. Also test Krea's upscaler / "enhance" on the 720p result
    (price it first).

C4  Try cheaper video routes for part of the film.
    ElevenLabs' hosted Veo 3.1 Fast produced the shot-31 redo for USD 0.97, while Krea Veo 3.1 costs
    1,185 units, about USD 2.07 at the Max rate. If quality is equal (never compared side by side), the 16
    Veo clips would have cost about USD 17.6 less. Caveat: credits in different plans are not
    interchangeable, and the real marginal cost depends on what the owner has already prepaid. Also check
    whether Krea itself offers a Veo Fast or Lite tier [U, not checked] and whether Kling 3.0 (never
    used) is cheaper per second.

C5  Trim exploration images.
    76 images were made for 41 used (54 percent). Nine reference sheets x 4 options = 36 images; 2
    options each would save 18 images (about 2 to 4 percent of the bill; per-image cost is not measured).
    Six keyframe re-rolls and a 2K Parvati re-render could have been avoided by putting the clothing /
    location / Ganesha-identity clauses in the shared preamble BEFORE the first keyframe batch. Do the
    "preamble hardening" test on two difficult shots (Ganesha with Shiva; the dark evening shot) first.

C6  Design shots to the model's clip length.
    Both models are priced by the second (Seedance about 421 units per second at 1080p, Veo about 148),
    but Veo only comes in 4, 6 or 8 s. Most Seedance clips were generated 0.5 to 1.1 s longer than the
    net shot (crossfade handles), and Veo clips were stretched to fill. Write each beat to a length the
    model makes natively (7.5 s net for Veo; any length from 4 s for Seedance) so no seconds are paid
    for and thrown away or time-stretched.

C7  Use cheap, non-AI motion for genuinely static moments.
    Establishing or scenic shots (for example shot 05, a wide view with a drifting cloud) could be 2.5D
    parallax from the keyframe (a free local depth-map tool plus ffmpeg or HyperFrames), saving about
    1,185 units each. Do this only where nothing needs to act; it lowers animation quality, so treat it
    as a budget lever, not a recommendation.

C8  ElevenLabs hygiene.
    - Always pass generations_count=1 (the default is 4 takes) [done in this project].
    - estimate_only runs high (music 7,075 vs 4,245; SFX 1,419 vs 513): budget from past actuals.
    - estimate_only left seven empty flows in the workspace (clutter, no cost); reuse one flow id.
    - Poll creative_get_flow, not the run-status call that returns about 14,000 tokens.
    - Audition at most two voices, with 15 s samples (the 3-voice audition cost 594 credits, USD 0.12).
    - Generate a 60 to 90 s loopable music bed rather than a 225 s one (saves about 2,000 credits, USD 0.40).
    These amounts are small. ElevenLabs is only 2 percent of the spend, so the real recommendation is
    the opposite: spend some of the Krea savings on MORE audio takes (see 15C).

C9  Re-spend savings where quality shows. The savings from C1 to C4 (USD 55 to 100) would pay for:
    animating shots 23, 27, 28 on Seedance (about USD 20), a lip-sync pass (price unknown, see B3), three
    native 9:16 clips for the Shorts (about USD 6 on Veo), several music takes, and a better 20 s end
    background.

C10 LLM-side cost (not credits): long image reviews and the 14,000-token status polls were the heavy
    items. Write small contact-sheet and measurement helpers early (preview.py was added late).

15B. ANIMATION IMPROVEMENTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B1  Animate shots 23, 27 and 28 (about 25 s, 9 percent of the runtime).
    They carry the emotional payoff: Kartikeya's "How did you win?", the sharing of the mango, the hug.
    They were PLANNED for Seedance and only moved to Veo to save credits; Veo then refused them. Seedance
    accepted all 12 of its shots. Cost at 1080p about 3 x 3,791 = 11,400 units (about USD 20); at 720p
    about 4,600 (USD 8). Alternatives: Kling 3.0 (never tried; a prompting guide exists); split each shot
    into two single-character shots that Veo may accept; reword the prompt (the refusal reason is not
    given by the API). Keep the existing push-in as a fallback.

B2  Remove the slow-down.
    16 of 17 Veo clips are time-stretched (6.25 to 20 percent) with frame duplication (setpts), plus
    held frames in shots 31 (0.9 s) and 33 (10.4 s). Symptoms to look for in playback: slight judder on
    moving subjects, floaty motion, characters appearing slower than their voices. Fixes, cheapest first:
      (a) write each Veo beat for 7.5 s net (8.0 s minus the 0.5 s crossfade) so the clip is used at
          1.0x (or at most 1.0625x);
      (b) use motion interpolation when stretching (ffmpeg minterpolate, or an AI frame interpolator
          such as RIFE; both untested here) instead of duplicating frames;
      (c) for beats of 9 to 10 s, make two Veo clips (first 4 s + last 4 s with the last frame of the
          first as the next start image) or use Seedance, which can make any length from 4 to 30 s.

B3  Lip-sync for the 10 character lines (7 Mooshak, 3 Ganesha) and the narrated "I have an idea" and
    "They are my whole world" beats (shots 02, 17, 24 were flagged in the shot list).
    ElevenLabs lists lip-sync models (sync-lipsync-v3, veed-lipsync-v2). Price was never obtained;
    run estimate_only first. For 4 to 5 year olds this is a polish item, but mouths that move without
    matching the words are the clearest "AI-made" tell in the film.

B4  Grade and match the look.
    Veo and Seedance clips and the three stills were joined with no colour matching; the owner-visible
    difference seemed small, but it was not measured. Add a light per-shot grade (match black point,
    saturation, white balance to a hero shot) in ffmpeg (curves, colorbalance) or in DaVinci Resolve.
    Shot 21's thatched hut (not in other shots) cannot be fixed by grading; re-generate it with a
    cleaner keyframe if the shot is re-done.

B5  Fix shot 21 properly.
    Re-generate with prompt wording that names no numbers or counting ("clear open blue sky, nothing
    in the sky"); do not ask for "space for numerals" in the sky. Veo has no negative-prompt field, so
    use positive phrasing. This removes the residual digit specks near 2:46 to 2:47. Combine with the
    cleaner hut.

B6  Vary the editing rhythm.
    All 32 cuts are 0.5 s crossfades and shots last 6 to 10 s. For toddlers this is calm, but it is also
    monotonous. Use cuts on action for the race and the circling, dip to a warm colour for the move
    from morning to evening, and some 4 to 5 s shots. Add one or two hold-and-reaction shots (for
    example Kartikeya's surprise).

B7  Add life to any remaining still or held frame.
    A light HyperFrames layer (drifting petals, slow sparkles, soft vignette breathing) over the stills
    and over shot 33's held frame costs no credits and hides the "frozen" feel.

B8  End-screen background (20 s).
    Currently an 8 s clip slowed 1.2x plus a 10.4 s held frame with a slow push-in. Make one 20 s
    Seedance clip (about 8,400 units at 1080p, 3,400 at 720p) or a seamless 8 s Veo loop (reverse and
    blend the last frame onto the first) so the characters keep waving for the full 20 s.

B9  Native 9:16 for the Shorts.
    Currently a 16:9 picture on a blurred background (about 31 percent of the 1080x1920 canvas shows the
    story). Generate 3 or 4 hero moments natively in 9:16 from re-framed keyframes (about 4 x 1,185 units
    on Veo) and cut them with the existing captions and end card.

B10 Consistency checks as a pipeline step.
    Characters stayed on-model mostly because keyframes were approved by eye one by one. An automatic
    check (count faces / characters per frame, compare to the expected cast; flag extra people like the
    shot-31 girl) would catch drift earlier and cheaper.

B11 Consider 4K Veo for the master (optional).
    Krea lists 4K on Veo 3.1 [per the model schema read at the start; price not checked]. Uploading
    a 4K file gives YouTube's higher-bitrate encodes even to a 1080p viewer; but the cost and the file
    sizes rise a lot. Low priority.

15C. AUDIO IMPROVEMENTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A1  HUMAN LISTEN-THROUGH (highest value, free). Check with headphones and with phone speaker:
      - the narration pace and whether the 0.92 time-stretch sounds natural;
      - the music level under the voice and at the transitions (219 s, 262 s);
      - the splice in the sung song ("Morya" joined after 10 beats were removed) and its vocal quality;
      - the counting pops, wing flaps, footsteps and cheer (the wing flaps were slowed to half speed);
      - whether any effect or the song start is too loud for a child (nothing exceeds -1.5 dBTP, but
        loudness at -14 LUFS is not "quiet").
A2  Pronunciation and accent. Have an Indian-English speaker check: "Ganpati Bappa Morya", "Kartikeya"
    (Kar-ti-KAY-ya), "modak", "Kailash", "Amma", "Baba", "Shiva". Fix with phonetic respelling in the
    script text or a pronunciation dictionary and regenerate only the affected lines (about 1 credit
    per character, a few cents).
A3  Direct the performance. Narration was one take per line with default voice settings and no emotion
    direction. eleven_v3 accepts audio tags such as [softly], [warmly], [pause]; use them for the
    count-along pauses, the whisper at "Ganesha thought and thought...", and a bigger lift at
    "Hooray for Ganesha!". Generate 2 to 3 takes and pick (4 takes is the default; cost per line is
    cents).
A4  Pace. The brief asked for 110 to 120 wpm; the delivered narrator is about 135. The slow-down is
    done with atempo=0.92 after generation. Prefer asking for a slower read (voice speed setting or
    punctuation / tags) and keep atempo as a last resort, or use a better time-stretch (rubberband).
    The shot lengths were built around the stretched timings, so changing pace moves the whole cut.
A5  Rebuild the narration without generation loss. The final mix uses narration_review_v2_noIntro.mp3,
    which was itself built from MP3 line files: MP3 (ElevenLabs) -> atempo and loudnorm -> MP3 ->
    final mix -> AAC. Build the narration bus straight from the original line files into a 24-bit WAV.
    Also, loudnorm is applied separately to each of the 59 lines, in its dynamic one-pass mode, on clips
    of 1 to 3 s; that can pump and set slightly different levels per line. Use a fixed gain per speaker
    (measure the average, apply one number) plus one overall compressor and the final two-pass
    loudnorm.
A6  Music. One take per track. Generate 2 to 3 takes of the story bed (a 60 to 90 s version is
    cheaper) and the song, compare, pick. Score to the picture: a gentle rise on "Off he went", a
    lighter feel for the flight (chapter at 1:34), a warmer cue at the lesson. Check licence
    terms for commercial use and for YouTube Content ID claims on AI music [U].
A7  Sung song. A synthetic singer is the least testable part of the film. Options: a human singer,
    or a new generation with a child-friendly vocal timbre and a repeat of the "Morya" call-and-response
    that the child can follow; verify the cut sounds musical at the splice.
A8  Event-driven sound effects. The 41 cues were timed from the script and narration. Re-time the key
    ones (wing flaps, footsteps, landing, mango split, claps, hop) from the visible events in the
    clips (frame numbers can be read with ffmpeg and a contact sheet) so sounds land on the action.
A9  Stereo image and tone. SFX are mono and centred; narration is dual-mono centred; only music is
    stereo. Pan movement (peacock fly-over left-to-right), add a touch of room reverb on voices, and
    a gentle EQ (cut low rumble below 80 Hz on voices, a small presence lift around 3 to 4 kHz,
    de-ess) for a more finished sound.
A10 Dynamics for small ears. LRA is 8.2 LU. For toddler viewing, a narrower range (about 5 to 6 LU) and
    a lower peak ceiling for the cheer / claps cues is friendlier; the brief fixes the overall level at
    -14 LUFS, which is reasonable for YouTube.
A11 Consider a quieter bed under the counting segments so children can hear their own answers; and add
    1 to 2 s of silence ("space for the child") after each question in the tails of lines L14, L25, L37.
    (The script already has about 2 s pauses; check that music does not fill them.)

D1  Upload the MASTER, not the one-file download. The 91 MB copy is 2.6 Mbps; the master is 12 Mbps.
    YouTube re-encodes everything, so a low-bitrate source gives softer results. The master is in five
    90 MiB parts in 09_final/parts/ (join: cat part00..part04 > file; PowerShell: copy /b). Better
    delivery routes than splitting: GitHub Releases (2 GB per asset), Git LFS, or cloud storage. The

D3  Replace the long ffmpeg filter graphs with a timeline file (EDL / OpenTimelineIO) or move final
    finishing into DaVinci Resolve (grading, Fairlight audio, transitions) while keeping the AI assets

D4  Overlays. All are readable and safe-margined, but check them on a real phone at arm's length;

15E. PROCESS AND TOOLING LESSONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E1  Price first, plan second, batch third. (C2.)
E2  Use hand-over files. HANDOFF.md and log.md let the project survive two context losses and a
    container restart. Keep doing this; add a tiny status JSON updated by every script.
E3  Workflow / multi-agent runs consume the account's usage limit quickly (nine parallel overlay
    authors failed at the limit). Prefer one agent with a precise brief and contact-sheet previews, or
    fewer agents, and always write each agent's output to a file so a failure loses nothing.
E4  Parallelism limit: submit Krea jobs in waves of 12 or fewer (noted in the plan); this was respected.
E5  Large files: GitHub 100 MB per file; the assistant's file-send limit 30 MiB. Plan the delivery
    route (cloud storage or Releases) at the start, not at the end.
E6  Container limits: a single ffmpeg graph with many inputs ran out of memory (Shorts); build in
    segments or lower thread counts.
E7  Tool quirks to remember: Krea returns no cost; Veo has no negative-prompt field; Seedance outputs
    carry audio; ElevenLabs sound-effect lengths ignore the requested length (about 1 to 2 s unless
    the v2 model is used); refused requests are the only way to see Krea prices.
E8  Evidence discipline: record what is measured vs assumed (this report's tags) and keep a "not
    verified" list from the start.

   videos can be flagged. This is one original story with human direction, but a series should keep
   clear variation and original narration and writing [U, policy details change].
 - Licences: check the commercial-use terms for Krea (Veo and Seedance outputs; Veo adds an invisible
   SynthID mark), ElevenLabs (voices, music, sound effects; voice licences vary by plan), Canva, GSAP
   (free licence terms for commercial use) and Fredoka (SIL Open Font License) [U].
 - Child content safety: no frightening or sudden sound or image; no flashing; no fade under 0.25 s;
   comedy from the mouse only; loudness -14 LUFS, true peak -1.5 dBTP; no external links in the video.
 - Religious sensitivity. The deities are shown kindly and are never laughed at. The visual conventions
   (Ganesha orange with two arms and a broken right tusk; Kartikeya with one face on a blue peacock;
   Shiva with a small friendly cobra and a closed third eye) are recorded in 01_script/bible.md and were

 - Data: no personal data was collected. The repository holds only project files.

APPENDIX A. PROMPT RECIPE
====================================================================================================
Image-to-video prompt per shot (02_prompts/prompts.json):
   prompt_full = STYLE + Vivideo motion prompt + CHAR_* blocks + LOC_* block + props + negatives
   - STYLE: stylised 3D character animation, warm golden light, rounded shapes, bright, friendly.
   - Vivideo prompt: built with style animation_3d_character, mode image_to_video, 16:9, destination
     youtube, motion_strength balanced, shot duration; negative prompt "no text, no captions, no
     watermark, no distorted anatomy, no warped geometry, no duplicate objects, no jittery motion, no
     flicker, no blur, no identity drift, no morphing, no fast cuts, no flashing lights".
   - CHAR_* and LOC_* are copied word for word from 01_script/bible.md.
   - Seedance uses prompt_seedance (Krea's guide: "slow/gentle/soft" can make whole clips run in slow
     motion, so those words are replaced by "steady, smooth"). Veo uses prompt_full without reference
     images. Negatives are written into the prompt text because neither model has a negative field.
Keyframes: Nano Banana Pro 1K 16:9 with character and location sheets passed as image_urls and a
preamble that fixes clothing, location, and Ganesha's identity.
Settings: 1080p, 24 fps, audio off (Seedance's audio ignored), Seedance waves of 12 or fewer jobs.
